01How I work

I spend my day on the floor of an HVAC and powertrain cooling plant: watching stations run, timing them, and working out why the line paces slower than it should. The answer is usually work content sitting in the wrong place, material presented at the wrong height, or a standard nobody can hold at takt.

Once the cause is clear, the fix has to survive the next shift. That means drawn layouts, GD&T-informed specifications, visual controls and standard work — then checked back against the numbers a month later to confirm the gain held.

Cooling module cell rebalance Team lead +20% jobs per hour

Team

Six people across Production, Quality, Logistics and Engineering, plus two line operators on shift.

What we found

One station carried 13 seconds more work than takt allowed, so the whole cell paced to it and five operators waited.

What changed

Work content redistributed by MTM analysis, material moved to point of use, standard work rewritten to the new sequence.

Result

Jobs per hour up 20%, cycle time down up to 24%. Certified as my Green Belt project.

Overtime elimination, powertrain cooling Team lead $97,152 / year

Team

Cross-functional group with Production supervision, Logistics and Engineering; countermeasures tracked on a single A3.

What we found

Scheduled overtime was covering a balance problem, not a demand problem. Operator allocation had drifted from the current takt.

What changed

Stations rebalanced to MTM work content, allocation reset against takt, Lean improvements to material flow to remove the walk time that justified the extra hours.

Result

$97,152 in annual savings, held after follow-up cycle-time checks.

01DMAIC, step through it

Define

The cell could not hold demand and overtime was covering the gap. As team lead I wrote the charter with Production, Quality, Logistics and Engineering, fixed the boundaries of the study to the six-station cooling module cell, and set the target condition on an A3 everyone signed.

A3, project charter, SIPOC, stakeholder walk

Measure

Repeated time studies at every station across shifts, not a single sample. Work-content analysis broke each station into elements, and a value-stream map put the cell in the context of the material coming to it.

Time study, MTM work content, VSM, Gage R&R, Minitab

Analyse

Pareto on stoppage causes, 5 Whys on the two that mattered, and operator allocation checked station by station against takt. The bottleneck was not the slowest machine — it was where the work content had accumulated after an earlier line change.

Pareto, 5 Whys, Gemba, line-balance chart

Improve

Elements moved between stations to level work content under takt, material brought to point of use so operators stopped reaching and walking, and standard work rewritten to the new sequence with the operators who run it.

MTM rebalancing, point-of-use presentation, standard work, 5S

Control

Visual controls at the cell, countermeasures tracked to closure on the A3, and follow-up cycle-time checks a month later to confirm the gain had not drifted back. Handed to the line with a standard the shift could hold unsupervised.

Visual controls, A3 tracking, PDCA, problem-tracking charts
